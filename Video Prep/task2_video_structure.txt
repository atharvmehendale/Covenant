COVENANT COMPLIANCE ENFORCER AGENT
TASK 2 — DEMO VIDEO STRUCTURE PROPOSALS
Prepared 1 September 2026.

THE BRIEF, RESTATED
  - A PITCH, not a tutorial. Hard 5-minute maximum.
  - Lead with the problem.
  - Show the solution actually working.
  - Must cover: (1) what problem, (2) who it's for, (3) why it matters.
  - Slides, screen recording and voiceover all fine. No camera needed.

THE ONE CONSTRAINT THAT SHAPES EVERYTHING
A real review takes about a minute on the paced path (SEQUENTIAL_PACE_SECONDS =
20, three to four calls). You cannot afford 60 seconds of a spinner in a
300-second video. So: PRE-RECORD every run, then cut the dead time. Speed-ramp
the waiting and keep the agent's status messages visible — "Reader is reading
the public commitment", "Reasoner is judging whether the draft narrows the
commitment" — because those lines do narrative work for free. They show the
multi-agent structure without you having to explain it over a diagram.

Never record a live API call for the final cut. Groq's free tier can rate-limit,
and you have one shot.


==============================================================================
OPTION A — "THE CATCH" (RECOMMENDED)
==============================================================================
Structure: Problem -> the catch, in full -> proof it generalises -> the honest
limit -> close. One deep demo instead of a tour.

Total: 4:45, leaving buffer under the 5:00 cap.

--- 0:00-0:35 | THE PROBLEM (35s) ------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN: Two quotes, appearing one after the other. No app, no logo, no
architecture. Large type.
  First:  "...committing to becoming carbon negative by 2030 for all three
           scopes."                                    [labelled: PUBLIC PLEDGE]
  Then:   "'Carbon Negative Operations' shall be defined exclusively as Scope 1
           and Scope 2... Scope 3 emissions are expressly excluded."
                                                       [labelled: SECTION 7.2,
                                                        PAGE 34 OF A SUPPLIER
                                                        CONTRACT]
Then a single line: NOBODY LIED. THE DEFINITION JUST CHANGED.

NARRATION: A company makes a public promise. Months later a 40-page supplier
contract quietly redefines it — dropping Scope 3, the largest part. No word is
misspelled. Nothing is missing. A definition was rewritten. Search can't find
this. A diff can't find this. It gets signed.

WHY IT OPENS HERE: this is your strongest asset and it needs no setup. A judge
who sees those two quotes side by side already understands the product. Do not
spend this window on a title card.

--- 0:35-0:55 | WHO IT'S FOR, AND WHY IT MATTERS (20s) ---------------------
ON SCREEN: One slide, three short lines. Title: "Covenant — Compliance
Enforcer."
NARRATION: It's for in-house counsel and compliance officers — the people who
sign off on contracts against commitments their company has already made
publicly, at a volume nobody can read line by line. Why it matters: a public
climate or privacy pledge is a promise to regulators, investors and customers.
Contradict it in a private contract and it stops being a drafting error and
becomes a disclosure problem.

KEEP IT TO 20 SECONDS. This is the part every entrant over-explains.

--- 0:55-2:25 | THE CATCH, END TO END (90s) --------------------------------
This is the heart of the video. One document, all the way through, uncut in
feel even though it is edited.

ON SCREEN, in order:
  1. (~10s) The terminal, watcher already running. A .docx or .pdf lands in
     inbox/. Caption: "Nothing was clicked. It watches."
  2. (~20s) The agent status lines appearing in sequence — Reader, Reader,
     Reasoner, Writer. Speed-ramped. Caption over them: "Reader extracts.
     Reasoner judges. Writer drafts. A lead agent decides who to call."
  3. (~10s) [FLAGGED] appears in the terminal.
  4. (~30s) Cut to the dashboard. Open the case by its NAME — "Matter: Pipeline
     Venture JV — Reporting Scope Review". Scroll slowly through the case view
     in its designed order: What's the issue -> the two quotes side by side,
     green vs red -> Suggested fix.
  5. (~20s) Rest on the Approve / Reject buttons. Do not click yet.

NARRATION over 4-5: It reads both documents, quotes the conflicting language
from each, and drafts the replacement clause. Then it stops. The fix is a
proposal. Approving records a human decision — it never edits the contract.

THEN, the beat that sells the whole design (part of the 90s):
Drop a CLEAN draft in. The watcher prints [CLEAR] and nothing else happens.
NARRATION: A clean contract produces silence. No memo, no alert, no case to
dismiss. A tool that flags everything gets ignored within a week.

--- 2:25-3:20 | IT ISN'T TUNED TO ONE EXAMPLE (55s) -----------------------
The credibility section. This is what separates you from a demo that works on
one hand-written file.

ON SCREEN: Split screen. Left, the emissions case. Right, the privacy case —
  Public: "does not sell, rent or monetize your personal data or content in any
           way - ever."
  Draft:  permits sharing with "trusted commercial partners... in exchange for
           consideration."
Then the real logged AI output, on screen as text:
  "The policy's absolute prohibition ("does not ... ever") is overwritten by a
   clause that explicitly permits "share, license, and otherwise monetize ...
   in exchange for consideration.""
Caption, large: SAME AGENTS. SAME PROMPTS. NO CODE CHANGES.

NARRATION: Both of those commitments are real published corporate wording,
reproduced verbatim under fictional company names. The second one is the point:
nothing in this system knows what an emission is. The component that reads the
verdict contains no subject vocabulary at all — it recognises the shape of a
walked-back promise, not a topic. So the same Reasoner that catches a dropped
emissions scope catches a privacy promise being sold off.

THEN (~15s of this section) the unreadable case:
ON SCREEN: upload the scanned PDF. The review refuses to start; the violet
"Could Not Be Read" case appears.
NARRATION: And when it can't read a document, it says so. A scanned contract is
never passed to the AI as an empty page, because the most dangerous thing a
compliance tool can output is a confident "no issues found" about a document it
never actually read.

*** DO THIS FIRST: run monitor.py once over
    samples/jv_texas_refinery_scanned.pdf so a real COULD_NOT_READ case exists
    on the dashboard. Currently that path has only ever been exercised by unit
    tests, so there is nothing to film. Two minutes of work for a much stronger
    beat than describing it. ***

--- 3:20-4:00 | BUILT PROPERLY (40s) --------------------------------------
Not a code tour. Two claims, each with something on screen.

ON SCREEN: the architecture diagram (CORRECTED — see the warning below), then a
terminal running the offline test suite to a clean pass.
NARRATION: Four Strands agents — a lead Orchestrator that's handed the Reader,
Reasoner and Writer as tools and decides itself which to call. Twenty-two
offline tests, no API calls, covering multi-page PDFs, Word tables, encrypted
files, corrupt files and the scanned case.

Then the one war story — it is worth 15 seconds on its own:
NARRATION: Real testing found real bugs. The best one: the retry loop was
causing the failure it was retrying. A Strands agent appends each prompt to its
own history, and a failed call doesn't undo that — so every retry re-sent all
the previous attempts. A 5,500-token request became 33,000 tokens and got
rejected for being too large. The fix was to rewind the conversation before each
retry so every attempt is the same size as the first.

*** WARNING: do not show Documentation/architecture_diagram.svg as it stands.
    It says "deployed on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore" and "Amazon Nova Pro" in four
    places. The code has PROVIDER = "groq". In an AWS-sponsored hackathon that
    reads as claiming a deployment you don't have. Relabel the model layer
    "Groq — gpt-oss-120b (pluggable: Bedrock wired up, not yet deployed)" and
    mark the AgentCore boundary as a planned target, or use a simple 4-box
    slide instead. See task1 file, section D2. ***

--- 4:00-4:30 | THE HONEST LIMIT (30s) -----------------------------------
Do not skip this. Judges have seen forty polished demos; the one that names its
own ceiling is the one they believe.

ON SCREEN: One slide. "Known limit: the free tier, not the design."
NARRATION: One real constraint. The full Orchestrator packs both documents and
every intermediate result into one large call — past the free tier's
8,000-tokens-per-minute ceiling on a realistically long contract. So there's a
paced path: the same agents, same prompts, called one at a time. Amazon Bedrock
removes that ceiling and the provider is already wired up behind a single
config switch. That's the next step, and it's a config change, not a rewrite.

--- 4:30-4:45 | CLOSE (15s) ----------------------------------------------
ON SCREEN: back to the two opening quotes, then the Approve/Reject buttons.
NARRATION: It reads what you promised, it reads what you're about to sign, and
it tells a human when those two things stopped matching. It never signs
anything. That's the point.

WHY OPTION A IS THE RECOMMENDATION
  - It leads with the problem in its most compressed possible form: two quotes.
  - It gives 90 unbroken seconds to ONE document end to end, so a judge sees a
    complete outcome rather than four partial glimpses. Depth beats coverage in
    a pitch.
  - The generalisation section lands AFTER the viewer is already convinced the
    single case works — which is the order in which scepticism actually arrives.
    Nobody asks "does this generalise?" until they believe it works once.
  - The honest-limit section is placed late, where it reads as confidence rather
    than as a disclaimer.
  - It never shows the codebase as a codebase, which is what makes a pitch feel
    like a tutorial.

  0:50-1:40  DEMO 1, EMISSIONS. Full walk through the case view. Then the clean

  1:40-2:30  DEMO 2, PRIVACY. Same beats, deliberately identical framing, so the

  2:30-3:10  THE SAFETY BOUNDARY. Approve/Reject; approving records a decision
             and never edits a contract. Then the scanned PDF being refused.
  3:10-4:10  HOW IT'S BUILT. Architecture, the 22 offline tests, the retry-loop
             bug, the content-fingerprint detail (an edited draft resaved under
             the same name is re-reviewed, because that's how negotiation works).
  4:10-4:40  THE HONEST LIMIT + BEDROCK NEXT STEP.
  4:40-4:55  CLOSE.

WHEN OPTION B IS BETTER: if you think the judges' main doubt will be "this only
works on the one example you built it for." The mirrored structure attacks that
directly and the symmetry is satisfying.

WHY I DIDN'T RECOMMEND IT: two full demos in a 5-minute pitch costs about 100
seconds of screen time for a point that Option A makes in 40 with a split screen
and one quote of real AI output. That 60 seconds is better spent on the single
end-to-end walkthrough. The cold open is genuinely strong though — see the
hybrid note below.

OPTION C — "THE TRUST ARGUMENT" (a considered alternative)
==============================================================================
Structure: lead with the design philosophy rather than the catch.

  0:00-0:30  THE THESIS. "An AI that reviews contracts has exactly two ways to
             be useless: it cries wolf, or it says everything's fine about a
             page it never read. This one is built to do neither."
  0:30-1:00  THE PROBLEM. The two quotes.
  1:00-2:00  THE CATCH. One document end to end.
  2:00-2:40  SILENCE ON CLEAN DOCUMENTS. The clean draft. No memo, no alert.
  2:40-3:20  REFUSAL ON UNREADABLE DOCUMENTS. The scanned PDF; the violet case.
  3:20-4:00  HUMAN AUTHORITY. Approve/Reject, never auto-applies, enforced in
             code — config flag, fail-safe default, dashboard writes only to
             approvals.json.
  4:00-4:30  GENERALISATION + ENGINEERING, compressed. Privacy case as a single
             quote; tests and the retry bug in one line each.
  4:30-4:50  CLOSE on the thesis.

WHEN OPTION C IS BETTER: it is the most intellectually distinctive framing of
the three, and "silence and refusal are features" is a genuinely unusual thing
to build a pitch around. If the judging panel skews toward people who have
deployed AI tooling and been burned by alert fatigue, this is the one that
resonates.

WHY I DIDN'T RECOMMEND IT: it opens on an abstraction. Thirty seconds of thesis
before any concrete artifact is a real risk when a judge is watching submission
number thirty-one. It also compresses the domain-independence proof — your
second-strongest claim — into the weakest slot in the video.


==============================================================================
RECOMMENDATION
==============================================================================
Go with OPTION A, and steal Option B's cold open.

The one change worth making to Option A: put 12-15 seconds of silent screen
recording at the very front — file lands, status lines, [FLAGGED], the two
conflicting quotes — before the narration starts. Then go into the two-quote
problem slide. It costs you a few seconds from the "built properly" section and
buys proof-of-life in the first ten seconds, which is when a judge decides
whether to watch properly.

Ranking, if you want it: A > B > C. A and B are close; C is the boldest and the
riskiest.

     labelling is the biggest unforced credibility risk in the whole submission.
     See task1 file, section D2.
  2. Generate a real COULD_NOT_READ case: put
     samples/jv_texas_refinery_scanned.pdf through monitor.py once. That path has
     never run end to end, so there is currently nothing on the dashboard to film.
  3. Do NOT demo jv_solar_farm.txt. Your own log shows it returning CLEAR,
     FLAGGED, then CLEAR again on identical content eleven minutes apart. Use
     the Meridian and Quillhaven cases, which landed correctly all four times.
     Details in task1 section D1.
  4. Make sure python-docx is installed before running the test suite on camera —
     one docx test silently skips without it.
  5. Do NOT run `python writer_agent.py` on camera. Its __main__ block prints
     writer_output['redline'], but with FAKE_MODE = False the real path returns
     'output' — so it raises KeyError: 'redline'. The orchestrator paths are
     unaffected (they read .get("output") or .get("memo")), so demo through
     monitor.py, orchestrator.py or the dashboard. Worth fixing anyway: it's a
     one-line change and a judge browsing the repo might try to run it.
  6. Do a full dry run of every command you intend to film, then film from the
     already-warm state.

CLAIM HYGIENE (all verified — say these, and only these)
  - "22 offline checks, no API calls" — correct: 17 in test_document_intake.py
    plus 5 in test_contradiction_checker.py, both with real assertions. But only
    the intake suite exits non-zero on failure; the contradiction suite prints
    its failures and exits 0. So don't claim both would fail a CI build.
  - Call run_real_document_test.py a "verification run against real published
    wording", NOT a test suite. It prints CHECK rather than FAIL and does not
    exit non-zero.
  - If you film the paced path, say "the same agents called in a fixed order for
    the free tier." Don't narrate "the model chooses its own sequence" over the
    sequential path — that's only true of the Orchestrator path.
  - Say the commitments are "real published corporate wording under fictional
    company names." Never name or imply the real companies.
  - Don't say "reliable", "consistent" or "reproducible" about verdicts.
  - If you mention the rule-based fallback at all, call it "a narrow
    emissions-specific offline baseline." It is three scope regexes and six
    hardcoded phrases; it cannot handle the privacy case.

TIMING DISCIPLINE
  - Hard-cut every wait. Never show a spinner for more than 2 seconds.
  - Keep the agent status lines legible — they explain the architecture for free.
  - Record narration separately and cut the visuals to it. Live narration over a
    live demo always runs long.
  - Target 4:45. A video that runs to 5:01 can be disqualified on a hard cap.

The dashboard case view with the public commitment in green on the left and the
